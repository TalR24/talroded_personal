About

I'm a data scientist, economic researcher, and policy writer. My work sits at the intersection of rigorous quantitative analysis and clear public communication — turning large datasets and economic models into findings that non-specialist audiences can act on. I work primarily in Python, R, SQL, and Stata, and I've applied those tools to problems ranging from AI's macroeconomic footprint to why NYC's streets aren't getting safer fast enough.

My day job is at LinkedIn, building data science systems at platform scale. Alongside that, I hold an affiliate researcher position at MIT FutureTech, where my research examines AI progress and its economic implications — benchmarking generative AI forecasts against prior technology cycles and analyzing the trends in training data that drive capability gains.

Before that I was a Senior Analyst at Accenture Research, producing quantitative research on generative AI's labor market impact for enterprise and policy audiences. Earlier, as a Research Associate at the Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia, I contributed to peer-reviewed work on macroeconomics, housing markets, and employment — applying econometric methods (panel data, regression discontinuity, difference-in-differences) to policy-relevant questions.

I studied Joint Math-Economics at UC San Diego (B.S., 2019), graduating with Provost's Honors. There I co-authored research on WWII-era unemployment dynamics and published work on income-education gradients in developing nations.

My policy writing lives at NYCuriosity — a Substack where I apply the same analytical rigor to NYC governance: transit funding gaps, street safety mandates, Community Board votes, and parking politics. I also co-founded Menti, a nonprofit where I built interactive data exploration tools in R Shiny and authored resources on education and career development, reaching 6,000+ monthly visitors. I'm a member of Technologists for the Public Good.
